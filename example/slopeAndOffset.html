Slope in Percent and Trend Offset

Two ways to make the label mean something — and to leave data out of the fit

Slope as a percentage

percentage: true

percentage prints the slope × 100. That is only a meaningful percentage when the y values are fractions of a whole and x is a real unit: here the share of households adopting a service goes from 5 % to 13 % over eight years, and the label reads 1.15 % per year.

  • On a category axis (regions, products) the slope is "per step", and × 100 names no unit — leave percentage off there
  • The y axis shows percent through a tick callback; the data stays 0–1
  • x is a linear axis with {x, y} points, so one x step is one year, not one data point

Leaving data out of the fit

trendoffset: -2

The last two quarters jump away from the series. trendoffset: -2 excludes them from the fit — and, because the line runs from the first to the last included point, it visibly stops before them.

  • Positive offset skips the first n elements: trendoffset: 2 starts at the third
  • Negative offset excludes the last n elements: trendoffset: -2 stops two from the end
  • An offset at least as large as the dataset is ignored, so a short series still gets a line
  • Works the same for a plain number array as for {x, y} objects — this chart uses numbers
